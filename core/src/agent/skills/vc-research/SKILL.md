# VC research

This workflow supplies domain judgment, not an execution budget or a source
list. Respect the selected light/deep mode, available tools and source scope.
Use the shared research skill for discovery mechanics and durable notes when
needed; do not duplicate its bookkeeping or add another completion gate.

- Identify the current job: opportunity discovery by default; meeting preparation
  or diligence only when requested. Do not automatically run every stage.
  Infer useful starting directions without inventing stage, geography, funding,
  check size or traction requirements. Follow-ups address the new request and
  affected findings, not a restart of completed work.
- For sourcing, find people worth learning about: demos, repositories, early
  users, hiring, cofounder requests, unusual approaches and practitioner
  discussions. Explore complementary directions and follow unexpected relevant
  signals. Unknown identity, funding or revenue is a label, not an admission
  gate. Keep useful small and unnamed projects alongside the shortlist.
- Build an opportunity map around problems, approaches, builders and emerging
  signals. Include a compact candidate list with why each matters, an observed
  source link, uncertainties and a worthwhile next step. Scale detail to the
  request and budget; do not require a large report or graph for every answer.
- Connect people, projects and organizations using observed links. Distinguish
  author, founder, employee, curator and guest. Preserve uncertain identity
  matches as hypotheses; do not merge on name alone. Show a relationship table
  or graph only when useful, with sources for asserted relationships.
- With multiple allowed platforms, follow promising links across them when
  useful: a demo can lead to a repository, a builder profile or an interview.
  Do not verify every candidate on every platform. Reposts are not independent
  confirmation. A podcast description is not a listened-to transcript; a search
  preview is not a full read. Separate source claims, observed activity and
  inference, with relevant dates and contradictions.
- For tracking, suggest specific signals, sources and a sensible cadence. These
  are recommendations, not a running monitor. For meeting preparation, suggest
  focused questions tied to unknowns; draft outreach only when requested and
  never send or contact anyone without explicit authorization.
- For explicit diligence, investigate the user's decisive questions and keep
  missing evidence visible. Do not promote a promising lead to a verified,
  investable or fundraising company. Discovery does not require diligence first.
